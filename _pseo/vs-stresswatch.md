  and surfaces it on your wrist, often within the hour. When your chest tightens during a meeting,
  the complication confirms it. That feedback loop is useful.

  What it doesn't always answer is whether today's spike is isolated or part of a longer slide.
  Chronic overload rarely announces itself as one bad afternoon. It accumulates — slightly worse sleep,
  slightly lower HRV, slightly higher resting heart rate — until a normal Tuesday feels impossibly heavy.

  Apple Watch stores the history that explains the difference between a moment and a pattern.
  The question is whether your app reads backward as well as it reads right now.
