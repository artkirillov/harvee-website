## SE 2: the entry watch that already does HRV

Apple Watch SE (2nd gen) updated the chip, added crash detection, and kept the price below Series. For health-curious buyers, it's often the best dollars-per-sensor ratio: overnight HRV, sleep tracking, heart rate, activity — without paying for ECG or titanium you may never use.

The trap is treating SE 2 as a stepping stone. "I'll get the real health watch later." Later never comes, and months of HRV in Health go unread. SE 2 already writes the signal Harvee needs. The stepping stone is interpretation, not hardware.

### Why SE 2 owners are ideal Harvee users

SE 2 buyers skew newer to Apple Watch health data. They want clarity, not jargon. They didn't buy a sports lab on their wrist — they bought a watch that tracks sleep and closes rings. Harvee's Playbook and Today tabs are built for that profile: one daily insight, education when you're curious, depth in Patterns when you're ready.

Passive overnight HRV fits real life better than morning measurement rituals. Parents miss mornings. Students snooze through alarms. SE 2 on your wrist while you sleep means Harvee's trend line stays intact even when your schedule doesn't.

### SE 2 vs Series: what you actually give up

You give up ECG, always-on display tiers, and some advanced sensors on certain models. You don't give up overnight HRV for Harvee's purposes. Comparative studies and real-world usage both suggest trend monitoring from watch HRV works when wear is consistent — SE 2 delivers that consistency if you sleep with it on.

### Building your baseline on SE 2

Week one: wear to sleep, open Harvee briefly each morning. Week two: baseline stabilises, deviations become meaningful. Week three: you can look back at a hard stretch and see when HRV started sliding — often before you named it stress.

That's the puzzle Harvee helps solve: not what is my number today, but what has my nervous system been doing while I was busy living.
